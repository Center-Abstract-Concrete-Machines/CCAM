A.J. McClenon was born and raised in D.C. proper in a multigenerational home with grandparents from Trenton and Chester, South Carolina. Currently, A.J. is a Chicago-based interdisciplinary artist and educator whose practice moves through text, repurposed materials, moving and still images, performance, and sound.

Familial and collective grief, water, Blackness, geomorphology, and the global future drive A.J.'s work, which is also grounded in abolitionist thought. How do systems of carceral and psychic confinement shape lived experiences? How does understanding these systems more deeply reimagine ecological pathways toward liberation?

Drawing from physics, psychology, and language, A.J. explores how time, ecology, and the decentering of humanness overlap, examining the earth as a living archive and sonic instrument. Through transmasculine embodiment and performance, the work navigates shifting relations to the body, the land, and time. 

A.J. McClenon has shown work and performed at places like the MCA Chicago, the National Museum of African American History and Culture, and the Experimental Media and Performing Arts Center. A.J. McClenon is currently an Assistant Professor, Adjunct at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago, and does teen programming at Columbus Park in the Austin neighborhood of Chicago.
